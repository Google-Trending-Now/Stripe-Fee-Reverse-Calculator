How Much to Charge to Cover Stripe Fees (The Exact Math)

Most freelancers try to pass Stripe fees along by adding 2.9% and $0.30 to their invoice. It looks right on paper, yet you'll still end up short when the payout hits your bank account.

Why? Because Stripe doesn't calculate 2.9% on your original rate. It takes 2.9% of the entire amount your client pays—including the extra fee you just tacked on. You end up paying a fee on the fee.

To figure out how much to charge to cover Stripe fees down to the exact cent, divide your target payout (plus the fixed fee) by 1 minus the percentage rate:

Total to Charge = (Desired Net Payout + Fixed Fee) ÷ (1 − Percentage Rate)

Example 1: You want to keep $100.00 (US rate)

If you just add 2.9% + $0.30, you'd bill $103.20. Stripe then deducts 2.9% of $103.20 plus $0.30 ($3.29), leaving you with $99.91—nine cents short.

($100.00 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.029) = $100.30 ÷ 0.971 = $103.30
Stripe fee on $103.30 = $3.30 → You keep $100.00

Example 2: You want to keep $1,000.00 (US rate)

Simple markup math gives $1,029.30. Stripe charges $30.15 on that total, dropping your payout to $999.15. Using the reverse formula fixes the gap cleanly.

($1,000.00 + $0.30) ÷ (1 − 0.029) = $1,000.30 ÷ 0.971 = $1,030.18
Stripe fee on $1,030.18 = $30.18 → You keep $1,000.00

Practical Billing Questions (FAQ)

Is it legal to pass Stripe processing fees on to clients?

It depends on where your client lives and whether you're billing consumers or businesses. In the US, most states allow credit card surcharges (capped at 3% to 4% by card networks) if you disclose them upfront, though Connecticut, Massachusetts, and Puerto Rico restrict them and debit card surcharges are banned nationwide. In the UK and EU, consumer card surcharges are illegal under PSD2. Australia caps surcharges at your actual cost of acceptance, and Canada permits credit card surcharges with advance notice outside Quebec. In practice, most experienced consultants don't add a "credit card fee" line item at all—they build the ~3% processing cost directly into their quoted project price.

How much should I charge to cover Stripe fees on international cards?

When a client pays with a card issued outside your country, Stripe tacks on an extra 1.5%. If Stripe also converts the payment into your local currency, expect another 1.0%. For a US business, that turns a 2.9% + $0.30 domestic card into 4.4% + $0.30 (international only) or 5.4% + $0.30 (international plus FX conversion). Tick the two matching add-on checkboxes above before sending cross-border quotes.

Does Stripe return processing fees if I refund a client?

No. Stripe keeps the original processing fee on both full and partial refunds. If a client pays a $1,030.18 invoice and you later refund the full amount, the client gets $1,030.18 back while you eat the $30.18 fee out of pocket. Make sure your contract specifies non-refundable deposits before kicking off large projects.

Should I use ACH or bank transfers instead of cards for large invoices?

Yes—once an invoice passes $500, card fees add up fast. In the US, Stripe ACH Direct Debit costs 0.8% and caps out at $5.00 per payment. On a $5,000 retainer, that's $5.00 instead of $149.64. If you bill clients in Europe, the UK, or Canada, look at SEPA Direct Debit, BACS, or direct bank wires/EFT for big milestones.

Why is my actual Stripe fee slightly different from 2.9% + $0.30?

A few extra Stripe products layer small percentages on top of base card processing: manually typed cards add 0.5%, Stripe Invoicing adds 0.4% to 0.5%, and Stripe Billing for recurring subscriptions adds 0.7%. On the flip side, negotiated volume accounts can pay less. Pick "Custom Rate" in the dropdown above to plug in the exact numbers from your Stripe dashboard.
